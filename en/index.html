Which frame should I pick? What makes a good recording?
  • Place the camera side-on and perpendicular to the person; this is how trunk and arm angles are measured most accurately.
  • Record a full cycle from start to end (at least 2–3 cycles). Analyse the most demanding moments separately: the deepest bend, the highest reach, the moment the load is lifted.
  • Also record frequently repeated or long-held postures; a “typical” frame also tells you about the job.
  • Save frames of the same task under the same task name ; the Workstations page then shows the timeline and the worst value together.
  • If the right and left sides carry different loads (e.g. one-handed carrying), assess each side with separate frames; choose the “Assessed side” when saving.
  • In footage shot at an oblique angle, angles are measured smaller or larger than they are. The camera should be at roughly waist height and perpendicular to the plane of movement.

The three results differ; which one should I use?
  • REBA: Jobs that use the whole body and change posture often (warehouse, handling, maintenance, cleaning). Weighted towards legs and trunk.
  • RULA: Jobs dominated by arms, neck and trunk with little leg movement (assembly, desk work, checkout). Gives little weight to the legs.
  • OWAS: Not a detailed score for one frame; for quickly classifying many postures over a shift.
  • Put the method that fits the type of work in the report and keep the others as supporting information. If two methods are both high, that job is the priority.
  • One frame shows one moment: save the most demanding moment and the most frequent or longest-held posture separately; if the two sides work differently, save each with “Assessed side”.

Angles

If a box is empty, the angle is calculated from the points. If you type a value, that value is used (orange). Borderline label means the angle is within a few degrees of a threshold that changes the score: if the camera is not exactly side-on, or the frame is not the most demanding moment, the score can shift by one step; check with a goniometer if you can.

Observations · used by all three methods

Mark what a side-on frame cannot show: the weight of the load, trunk or neck twisting and side bending, arm abduction, raised shoulder, wrist deviation. If not marked, these are treated as absent and the score comes out lower than it should.

Structure: Site → Workstation → Task. Analyses are saved to a task; a workstation’s risk is the worst of the current measurements of its tasks.

Risk assessment table

Each workstation × risk area is one row. Values are suggested from the method result; adjust them to the site with “Edit”. The table goes into the Excel and PDF report as is.

Body regions and job rotation

How much each task loads each body region is derived from the saved analyses. Select tasks for rotation: the app suggests an order in which two consecutive tasks do not load the same region at a high level.

Export / import

The Excel report contains a summary, results and a separate sheet for each method; download a task’s own report from the task header and a single record’s assessment form from its record card. CSV is in long format (each row is one method result) and goes straight into an Excel or Power BI pivot. JSON is a full backup.

Excel download isn’t available in this view; you can use Copy CSV.

Actions

Take action → implement → re-measure the same task. When the re-measurement is saved, the before-after comparison appears here and on the workstation page.

Cost and payback
